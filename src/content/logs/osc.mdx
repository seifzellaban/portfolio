Software engineers measure bottlenecks and cut redundant work. For a year, I applied those same habits to **Human Resources**.

As Head of HR for the Open Source Community (OSC), I ran the department like a backend pipeline. Output jumped across the board, yet the leadership team viewed optimization as disruption. This is the record of the system we built and the mismatch that ended my term.

## Phase 1: The Build

I took over as Head of HR in October 2024 with one goal: scale. We had to process hundreds of applicants with a volunteer team accustomed to informal habits.

I built a structured hiring pipeline and wrote a "Rules of Engagement" document defining disciplinary tiers, rewards, and KPIs.

The first recruitment cycle delivered clear numbers:
* Total interviews rose from **127 to 248**, near double the prior cycle.
* We onboarded **164 candidates**.
* I conducted **37 interviews** alongside managing department operations.

Beyond hiring, I ran mentoring sessions each week on **KPI design** and **data analysis** so my team could run the system on their own.

## Phase 2: The Engineer in the Room

Friction started as a software engineering student stepping outside HR boundaries.

The Tech Committee stalled on a tool HR needed. I skipped the extra meetings, stayed up overnight, wrote the automation script, and shipped it.

I viewed that as initiative. Leadership viewed it as breaking the chain of command.

We cared about different things. I tracked working scripts, clean recruitment sheets, and candidate experience. Leadership tracked Discord activity, server tags, and process rituals. I was playing *Factorio* while they played *The Sims*.

## The Exit

Leadership and I hit an impasse and parted ways. They wanted an HR head focused on constant status updates and hierarchy. I wanted to ship tools and trim overhead.

> The fastest process OSC ran all season was my removal. A group that took weeks to approve a tool revoked my server access in under four minutes. I wish they brought that speed to their projects.

## The Lesson

I left OSC proud of the 17 people on my team and the recruitment records we set. We proved a student HR department can run with real operational discipline.

Culture fit still decides the outcome. Drop a racing engine into a tractor and the frame tears apart.

I am bringing my recruitment pipelines, operational frameworks, and live Linux troubleshooting to a group that values shipping over optics: [MSP](/logs/msp).
