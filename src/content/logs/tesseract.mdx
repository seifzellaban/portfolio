Code editors like VS Code, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot track repo context, suggest fixes, and handle boilerplate.

Writing and planning tools lag behind. Obsidian, Notion, and Apple Notes work well for storage, yet they sit passive. They store text without reading your tone or intent.

I am building **Tesseract** to bridge that gap.

Tesseract is a local-first Markdown editor for brainstorming and journaling, paired with a built-in **Sentiment Analysis Engine**. It reads your writing as you type and shifts its assistant to match your working state.

***

## Core Features

### 1. Mood-Aware Interface

A local AI model scans your draft in real time to detect mood and tone.

- **Journaling or venting:** If the model detects stress or burnout, the assistant switches to a reflective tone and asks coaching questions to help unpack the problem.
- **Deep work:** If the model detects technical focus, the assistant switches to short, factual, logical answers.

### 2. Dynamic Tooling

Based on detected intent, Tesseract launches dedicated tools inside the editor.

Type *"I need to plan my study schedule for the next 3 days."*

The assistant opens a **Time-Planning Tool**, asks for your available hours and pace, generates schedule options ("Intense Sprint" vs. "Balanced Pace"), and writes your chosen plan straight into the Markdown file as checkboxes.

### 3. Total Recall

Copilot indexes a codebase; Tesseract indexes your notes using local RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation).

Planning a week triggers reminders about unfinished tasks from last Tuesday. Drafting a project spec surfaces links to notes you wrote six months earlier.

### 4. The Council of Thought (Debate Mode)

Echo chambers hide weak ideas. Debate Mode puts a raw proposal in front of three AI personas with opposing goals:

- **The Visionary:** Pushes the upside and long-term potential.
- **The Skeptic:** Attacks weak assumptions, risks, and logical gaps.
- **The Pragmatist:** Focuses on execution, time, and engineering trade-offs.

Highlight a line like *"I should rewrite the backend in Rust"* and click **Debate**. The editor splits and streams a three-way argument so you can weigh the trade-offs and decide.

## Tech Stack

- **Frontend:** Electron + React (dark-mode UI)
- **Backend:** Python (local inference)
- **AI:** Ollama + custom sentiment analysis model
- **Privacy:** Runs 100% on your machine with zero cloud sync

## Origin of The Manuscript

Before naming Tesseract, I tested whether standard AI assistants could give real editorial feedback. I pasted a 3,000-word fiction draft into a chat window and got back: *"The scene has good pacing overall."* Zero concrete critique.

That led me to build **The Manuscript**, a developmental editing skill with seven sub-skills covering the fiction workflow. Building it showed me how structured schemas turn vague praise into useful diagnosis, and those lessons now drive Tesseract's architecture.
