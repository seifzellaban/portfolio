## The brief

repAI was built for the GDG Cairo AI Finance Hackathon in March 2025. The prompt sat at the intersection of two problems: recycling is inconvenient, and the financial incentive to do it is usually invisible.

Most recycling apps stop at information. They tell you what can be recycled and where, then leave the actual work to you. We wanted to close the loop — identify the item, make the action worth something, and show the reward immediately.

## What we built

repAI lets a user point a camera at an item and get an answer: is this recyclable, and what is it worth? The app uses Google AI for object detection, then maps the result to a rewards model with cashback attached to the recycling action.

The loop is deliberately short:

1. Scan an item.
2. Confirm what the model found.
3. See the reward.
4. Track what you have diverted from waste.

Short loops matter in sustainability products. If the reward takes a week to arrive, the habit never forms.

## The build

The app is a React frontend with Tailwind CSS, wired to Google AI for recognition and a lightweight backend for the rewards ledger. The hackathon timeline forced hard choices: we optimized for a demo that felt complete rather than a feature list that felt impressive.

That meant focusing on the scan-to-reward path and cutting anything that did not make that path clearer. It also meant designing the empty and error states early, because live demos rarely behave.

## The result

repAI placed in the **top 7 out of 88 teams** at the GDG Cairo AI Finance Hackathon.

More useful than the placement was the feedback: judges and other teams pushed on the incentive design, not the model accuracy. That told us the AI was good enough and the product question was the real one.

## What I took away

A hackathon is a forcing function for scope. The version we shipped in a weekend was not the version we imagined on day one, and it was better for it. Since then I have been more aggressive about cutting features that do not serve the core loop.
