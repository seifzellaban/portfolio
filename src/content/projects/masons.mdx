## The brief

Masons is a software studio that builds meaningful, impact-driven software. The site had one job: make that sentence believable in under a minute.

Most studio sites lean on the same furniture — a wall of logos, a vague "we build digital experiences" headline, and a contact form buried three clicks away. I wanted the opposite: a short, confident explanation of how we think, what we ship, and why the work matters.

## The approach

The design is deliberately quiet. Large serif headings carry the hierarchy, the palette stays neutral, and motion is used only to guide attention — not to decorate. Every section earns its place: positioning, philosophy, selected work, and a way to start a conversation.

The copy does most of the work. Instead of listing capabilities, it states a point of view: software should solve real problems for real people, and the craft matters in getting there.

## The build

The site is built on the Next.js App Router with Tailwind CSS and shadcn/ui for the component primitives. Motion handles the few transitions that need to feel alive, and the whole thing is statically rendered so it loads fast on the connections our audience actually has.

A few decisions that paid off:

- **Type-first layout** — the page still reads well with images disabled.
- **Reusable sections** — the same primitives now power other Masons surfaces.
- **Restrained animation** — small entrance transitions instead of a scroll-jacked showreel.

## What it changed

The site became the reference point for how Masons presents itself: direct, warm, and allergic to filler. It also gave me a place to test ideas — type scales, spacing, motion restraint — that I now reuse in client projects.

## Reflections

The hardest part of a studio site is resisting the urge to say everything. The first drafts tried to cover every service, every technology, every possible engagement. Cutting that down to a clear point of view made the site feel more like a studio and less like a directory.
